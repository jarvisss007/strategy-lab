How Many Retail Trading Strategies Survive Honest Evaluation? A Systematic Falsification Study

Anupam Patil · Working paper, July 2026 · Draft v0.2 (assembled from the project repositories; all numbers machine-generated by the cited code)

Code and data: github.com/jarvisss007 — backtest-overfitting (toolkit), strategy-lab (survey), dc-ml-trading, regime-monte-carlo (companion papers).

Abstract

Retail trading culture supplies an endless stream of strategies that look profitable in backtests. Academic finance has documented the mechanism — multiple testing, backtest overfitting, and execution-assumption errors inflate in-sample performance (Bailey & López de Prado 2014; Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2015) — but retail evaluations rarely apply the corrections. This paper applies a uniform "honest evaluation" gate (Deflated Sharpe Ratio, Probability of Backtest Overfitting via CSCV, minimum backtest length, cost-aware walk-forward, and strict execution realism) to a broad sample of strategies a retail trader would actually consider: nine classic equity strategy families over 15 years; a popular commercial TradingView "Smart Money Concepts" system; intraday timing, sentiment, mean-reversion, and event studies on S&P data; cryptocurrency order-flow signals; two folklore claims taken directly from best-selling price-action trading books; and fifteen years of Indian index weekly-options structures. The result is nearly uniform: no market-neutral or options strategy tested survives the gate. The only survivors are long-only variants of risk-managed equity beta, which add no deflation-robust alpha over buy-and-hold. Several strategies fail in instructive ways: a lookahead bug masquerading as edge (PBO 0.60), an edge that exists only in an untradeable execution window, statistically significant microstructure signals that are net-negative after costs, premium-selling structures that win up to 86% of weeks while compounding to losses, and a near-universally taught "wait for confluence" rule that degrades out-of-sample performance rather than improving it. We argue the honest-evaluation gate is cheap, reusable, and should be the default before any capital — and we document a pre-registered forward-testing protocol now running as the out-of-sample continuation of this study.

1. Introduction

The replication crisis in factor research (Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016; Hou, Xue & Zhang 2020) has a retail sibling that is larger and almost entirely unexamined. Strategy ideas reach retail traders through backtest screenshots — from social media, best-selling trading books, strategy marketplaces, and charting-platform script libraries — evaluated without multiple-testing corrections, without out-of-sample discipline, and frequently with subtle lookahead or execution errors. The tools to audit these claims have existed for a decade, but they live in institutional quant literature, not where retail decisions are made.

This paper does three things. First, it packages the audit machinery as a small open-source toolkit and validates it on synthetic data (§2). Second, it applies one uniform gate to a deliberately heterogeneous sample of strategies spanning what a motivated retail trader in 2026 actually encounters (§4–§8). Third, because even honest backtests remain vulnerable to selection at the researcher level, it registers a live forward-testing protocol whose predictions are timestamped, scored without discretion, and publicly aggregated (§9).

The contribution is not a new anomaly — it is the opposite: a documented, reproducible demonstration of how many popular strategies fail when evaluation is honest, and a catalogue of the specific failure modes that make them look like they work.

2. The honest-evaluation gate

A strategy claim passes the gate only if all of the following hold:

  1. Deflated significance. The Deflated Sharpe Ratio (DSR; Bailey & López de Prado 2014) corrects the observed Sharpe for the number of trials, non-normality (skew, kurtosis), and sample length, benchmarked against the expected maximum Sharpe of equally-many noise strategies. We require DSR ≥ 0.95.
  2. Low probability of backtest overfitting. PBO estimated by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation (CSCV; Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu 2015). We flag PBO > 0.10 and report the in-sample→out-of-sample rank degradation slope.
  3. Sufficient sample. Minimum backtest length (MinBTL) given the trial count; e.g., selecting among 1,000 configurations at target Sharpe 1.0 requires roughly a decade of data before the winner means anything.
  4. Execution realism. Next-bar-open fills, stop-loss-priority on ambiguous bars, completed-bar (non-repainting) indicator values, published-data lags (e.g., sentiment used only after its release date), and explicit transaction costs.
  5. Untouched holdout. Where a parameter grid was searched, a 70/30 temporal holdout the search never saw.

Toolkit validation. On 200 pure-noise strategies the best in-sample Sharpe is 1.34 — and the gate rejects it (DSR 0.36, PBO 0.50). A planted Sharpe≈3 edge among 199 noise strategies passes (DSR 0.998, PBO 0.04). The instrument distinguishes signal from selection.

3. Study 1 — Systematic survey of classic strategy families

Nine strategy families (eight substantive plus a day-of-week noise control), full parameter grids, 15 years of daily data (2011-07 → 2026-07), 119-ticker universe plus SPY, 5 bps per unit turnover, one-day trade lag. Benchmark: SPY buy-and-hold Sharpe 0.921 over the same window.

MA trend basket 8 1.20 1.00 0.12 1.47 survives*
Turn-of-month 3 1.09 1.00 0.08 1.21 survives*
Vol-managed SPY 6 0.99 1.00 0.37 0.95 survives*
Donchian breakout 6 1.19 1.00 0.57 1.35 suspect
52-week high/low 17 1.15 0.48 0.06 1.21 suspect

Short-term reversal 18 0.05 0.00 0.47 −0.74 suspect
Day-of-week (control) 5 −0.06 0.17 0.68 −0.56 overfit ✓control

The asterisk is the finding. Every formal survivor is a long-only construction whose returns are dominated by equity beta over a window whose out-of-sample segment (≈2022–2026) contains exactly one bear market. None demonstrates deflation-robust alpha over simply holding the index; the universe itself (today's watchlist) embeds survivorship bias, and selecting the best family across nine adds a layer of multiple testing the per-family gate does not count. The noise control behaving as noise is the quality check that the gate is not vacuously permissive.

Conclusion of Study 1: zero market-neutral edges; "what survives" is risk-managed beta.

4. Study 2 — Anatomy of a commercial strategy: the r17 conviction

"IE Pro r17" is a commercial TradingView Smart-Money-Concepts system (EMA/VWAP/RSI scoring plus liquidity sweeps, break-of-structure, fair-value gaps, order blocks). We ported it 1:1 to Python with honest fills and non-repainting indicator values, then evaluated it four independent ways:

  1. Causal replay. The TradingView backtest's profitability reproduces only when the platform's request.security lookahead behavior (seeing up to 14 minutes ahead) is reproduced. Causal evaluation collapses profit factor from 1.44 to 0.57 (short-only, pseudo-OHLC); raw sweeps on true OHLC test at t = −2.9. The backtest performance was the bug.
  2. Deflation of the audit sweep. A 6-configuration audit of the 0DTE variant: the in-sample winner was exactly the configuration with the lookahead error; DSR 0.34, PBO 0.60, IS→OOS slope −1.44 → overfit.
  3. Variant sweep. 11 strategy variants including inversions: best (fading r17) posts annualized Sharpe 3.24 — against an expected best-of-11-noise Sharpe of 3.62; DSR 0.42 → unproven.
  4. Condition and ML referendum. 38 entry conditions under triple-barrier labeling with Bonferroni correction: zero survivors on both 5-minute and hourly data. The single provisional survivor (near-prior-day-high, 64.9% up over n=131) was taken to a 7-year referendum: 779 events, 52.5% up (z = 0.88), net −0.225R (t = −6.2) — convicted. A LightGBM/logistic walk-forward layer on the full feature set: out-of-sample AUC within 2 standard errors of 0.5 — a coin flip.

One commercial system, four independent methods, one answer.

5. Study 3 — Breadth: the same gate across asset classes

  • Intraday timing (S&P, 302 sessions of minute data). The "session timing" folklore reduces to the long-documented U-shape: moves cluster at the open and close (up to 21.5% vs 10% baseline frequency, Bonferroni-significant) — a description of variance, not an edge; a 17-timestamp × 4-holding grid produces a best t of 2.68 against an expected max-noise t of 2.39, with zero Bonferroni survivors.
  • Daily mean-reversion (32 years). The pattern is statistically real (all nine down-streak longs positive; PBO 0.002) but fails deflation (DSR 0.59), and its adaptive walk-forward implementation earns +2.6%/yr (Sharpe 0.26) against buy-and-hold's +10.2%/yr (Sharpe 0.53). Real anomaly, no retail edge — a category this literature needs.
  • Sentiment timing (AAII, 39 years, publication-lagged). Best of 24 variants is "always long" (Sharpe 0.64 = the index); DSR 0.23; the flagship conditional strategy underperforms holding SPY on its own selected days.
  • Insider-cluster gap (SEC Form 4). Entry at the filing-day close yields net Sharpe +3.1 — but that close is not purchasable when filings arrive after hours. At the honest next-open entry, every variant is net-negative (all |t| < 2). The edge lives entirely inside an untradeable execution window — the cleanest demonstration we have that execution assumptions are a multiple-testing dimension of their own.
  • Crypto order flow (Coinbase, 187k trades). Order-flow imbalance predicts 5–60s returns with information coefficients of 0.12→0.03, permutation p = 0.002 at every horizon — genuinely significant — and every configuration is net −60 bps per trade after realistic costs (hit rates 12–23%). Statistical significance and money are different quantities.
  • ML on directional-change events (companion paper). Gradient-boosted filters on DC events across S&P, NASDAQ, WTI (1986–2019): no bootstrap p-value below 0.05; the draft literature's headline "+37.8%" WTI result lands at +1.4% under independent reimplementation.
  • Event studies (war escalation/de-escalation, 24 events). De-escalation drift of +142→+240 bps (bootstrap p ≈ 0.014–0.024 at 1d/5d) is suggestive, not conclusive at N = 8, and is reported as such rather than traded.

6. Study 4 — Fifteen years of index weekly-options structures

Using 783 weekly cycles of real BankNifty closes and real India VIX (2011–2026), with synthetic flat-vol Black-Scholes premiums (India VIX × 1.25, 6.5% rate, 1.5%/leg/side costs):

Structure Win rate Profit factor Ann. return Ann. Sharpe
Short 1σ strangle 73.6% 0.67 −6.4% −0.83
Iron condor 71.0% 0.76 −2.6% −0.74
Short ATM straddle 55.6% 0.72 −10.0% −0.84
Put credit spread 86.1% 0.89 −0.6% −0.24
Long ATM straddle 42.0% 1.15 +4.4% +0.37
Long index (no options) 53.9% 1.31 +13.5% +0.70

Every premium-selling structure loses after costs despite win rates up to 86% — the high-win-rate/negative-expectancy trap measured at scale, with settlement computed on real index paths. The best performer in the tournament is not trading options at all, and even it fails deflation once the six trials are counted (DSR 0.256; verdict: unproven). The lone positive options result (long straddles) sits precisely on the model's weakest assumption — flat volatility omits the smile premium real buyers pay — and is therefore recorded as a hypothesis for real-chain data, not a finding. Caveats cut both ways and are documented in the repository.

7. Study 5 — Trading-book folklore: does "confluence" beat a single signal?

Books are the oldest retail strategy channel and the most authority-coded — a reader trusts a paperback differently than a forum screenshot. Two claims from popular price-action titles (Price Action Breakdown; The Ultimate Guide to Price Action Trading) are concrete enough to run on the Study 1 apparatus: 15 years of daily data, 119 names plus SPY, 5 bps per unit turnover, one-day lag.

Claim (i) — value-area reversion. "Price reverts toward its value area." Operationalized as a cross-sectional fade of each name's distance from its 20-day mean (long the most-below, short the most-above), across six configurations (fade quantile ∈ {0.1, 0.2} × holding ∈ {5, 10, 21} days). The best config (q0.2, 21-day hold) posts in-sample net Sharpe 0.08 and walk-forward 4/5 — but out-of-sample net Sharpe −0.03 and DSR 0.16. The direction is real; the edge is not. Verdict: real-but-loses-to-buy-and-hold — the same category as the daily mean-reversion result of §5.

Claim (ii) — confluence. "Never trade a single signal; wait for confluence." This is arguably the single most repeated instruction in retail technical education, and it is directly testable: take the identical value-area fade, but count a position only where a longer-horizon trend agrees with it. If confluence is the edge the books claim, the gate should improve the fade. It does the reverse — every confluence configuration underperforms the ungated fade, walk-forward scores collapsing to 0–1 of 5. The confirming condition added no signal; it subtracted sample and supplied a narrative. Verdict: dead — refuted.

The second result is the transferable one. "Confluence" feels like discipline — more conditions, more confirmation, more patience — but in this universe the extra condition is a selection knob wearing the costume of prudence, and it makes the strategy strictly worse. We record it as a sixth failure mode, complexity comfort, in §8.

8. What survives, and the taxonomy of false edges

Across every study, the surviving set is: long-only equity exposure and its risk-managed variants — none of which demonstrates deflation-robust alpha over the index itself. The false edges, meanwhile, fall into six reproducible categories:

  1. Selection — the best of N trials reported as if N = 1 (r17 variants, timestamp grids, sentiment variants).
  2. Lookahead — indicator or data timing that quietly sees the future (the r17/request.security collapse; rebuilt forward-return labels that shrank a "+0.169% edge" to +0.009%).
  3. Execution fiction — fills at prices unavailable in practice (the insider-gap conviction).
  4. Cost blindness — significant signals that are net-negative after realistic frictions (crypto OFI; 0DTE momentum at an 11% pre-cost win rate).
  5. Distributional camouflage — high win rates concealing negative expectancy (premium selling, §6).
  6. Complexity comfort — confirming conditions layered on for the feeling of rigor ("wait for confluence") that add no out-of-sample signal, and here subtract it (the value-area confluence gate, §7).

9. The forward-test protocol (pre-registered, running)

Backtests — even honest ones — leave residual researcher degrees of freedom. Since July 2026, five live "agents" (US equities, Indian indices, insider clusters, 0DTE chains, crypto order flow) log falsifiable, timestamped predictions to append-only ledgers, each scored mechanically on its check date under a written no-discretion rule, with results pooled in a public scoreboard. Hit rates near coin-flip will be reported as findings, consistent with the rest of this paper. This converts the study from a retrospective audit into an ongoing pre-registered experiment.

10. Limitations

Single researcher; free data (Yahoo, EDGAR, exchange feeds) with their known defects; the survey universe embeds survivorship bias (stated where it binds); the options study uses synthetic premiums pending real chain data; several sub-studies have modest N (war events); the trading-book claims are tested only on this US large-cap daily cross-section, not the intraday charts their authors typically use, so their failure here refutes the stated logic in this universe rather than everywhere; and cross-study selection — the choice of which strategies to test — is itself a bias this paper cannot fully escape, only disclose.

11. Conclusion

Given one uniform, honest gate, the retail strategy landscape thins to almost nothing: beta survives, alpha claims do not. The practical implication is not nihilism — it is sequencing. The gate costs an afternoon; the strategies it rejects cost accounts. Evaluation this cheap should precede capital every time.
